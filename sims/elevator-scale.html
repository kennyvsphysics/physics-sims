How to use this simulator (students & teacher notes)
  • Arcade (purple panel): Heavier or Lighter? is 8 quick rounds: read the situation, watch the car, and tap MORE, SAME, LESS or ZERO before the timer runs out. Fast answers and streaks score more. Bullseye Launch is 5 rounds: calculate the acceleration that makes the scale hit a target reading, then launch to see how close you got. Both add points toward your rank.
  • Badges unlock for things like cutting a cable, saving a falling car, breaking a scale, pinning a rider to the ceiling or visiting 5 worlds. The Stomach-o-meter shows how the ride feels (scale reading ÷ true weight). ♫ Elevator music plays while the car moves.
  • Pick a passenger and a planet, then pick how the elevator moves: a Quick case, Drive it with sliders, or plan a Trip from one floor to another. Press ▶ Run.
  • The scale measures the normal force FN the scale pushes up on the rider. With up as positive, Newton’s 2nd law gives FN − mg = ma, so FN = m(g + a) = mg + ma.
  • Only the acceleration changes the reading, not the velocity. Moving at constant velocity (up or down) reads the same as standing still.
  • When a = −g (cable cut), the reading is 0: free fall feels weightless. If the car is pulled down even faster than g, the rider floats to the ceiling and the ceiling has to push down on them.
  • The “kg” setting shows what a bathroom scale calibrated on Earth would display (reading ÷ 9.81). On the Moon it shows a much smaller “kg” number, even though the mass hasn’t changed!
  • Predict mode hides the readings. Pick a phase, predict more/same/less/zero (and a number if you want), then check.
  • Regents shows FN = mg + ma. AP adds the full Newton’s 2nd law setup, g-force ratio, the ceiling force, brake strength and more challenges.
  • Teacher notes: masses are approximate. The scale is ideal (it reads the normal force instantly). Air resistance and buoyancy are ignored. The safety brake decelerates at a set rate (default 9.81 m/s², like the strongest real safety gear). The building has 100 floors, 4 m apart; the car stops itself at the top and bottom of the shaft. Quick cases use a = 2 m/s², a bit snappier than real elevators (1–1.5 m/s²). Settings, points and finished challenges are saved on this Chromebook (localStorage keys starting “elevsim:”).
